📖Reading text

Sam, 17, comes back from school excited about a Career Day where he discovered jobs nobody had even heard of a decade ago. Target language is in yellow bold.

"Dad, There Are Jobs You've Never Even Heard Of!"

★ Sam, 17 · After his school's Career Day

Last Friday, my school held a Career Day. Honestly, I was expecting the usual predictable line-up — lawyers, doctors, an accountant who would talk for forty minutes about tax. But it actually blew me away. Half the people who came in had jobs that didn't even exist when my parents were my age. When I told my dad about them later, he just stared at me and said, "Sam, are you sure these are real?" Welcome to 2026.

Take Mia, for example. She's 26 and she's a prompt engineer. In a nutshell, her job is to talk to AI all day — to write the perfect instructions so that systems like ChatGPT or Claude give back exactly what humans need. "I'm basically a translator between people and machines," she told us. She earns a six-figure salary, works fully remotely, and the funniest part? She didn't study computer science. She studied poetry. As she put it: "You'd be surprised how useful that turns out to be."

Then there was Tom, who's 31 and a commercial drone pilot. He doesn't fly planes — he flies small flying robots. One week he's filming weddings, the next he's delivering essential medication to remote villages, and the next he's checking the structural condition of skyscrapers. "No two days are the same," he said. He had a drone with him in the gym hall and let some of us fly it. I crashed it. Tom didn't seem to mind.

"If you'd told me ten years ago I'd make a living flying robots, I would have laughed in your face." — Tom, drone pilot

Anya was probably my favourite speaker. She's a video game designer at a small indie studio. She told us she got into the industry by making tiny games when she was twelve. Twelve! "If you're passionate about something," she said, "start now. Don't wait for permission. The internet is full of free tutorials and welcoming communities — the barriers to entry have never been lower." I'm definitely going to look into that this weekend.

Some of the speakers had jobs that didn't even sound like jobs at first. Lara, for instance, is a full-time content creator. She makes short cooking videos for TikTok and has 800,000 followers. People often assume it's easy money, but she set us straight. "It looks effortless on screen," she laughed, "but behind the scenes, I'm filming, editing, writing scripts and replying to thousands of comments — every single day. It's a grind. The most important asset in this job is being yourself. The minute you start copying others, your audience can smell it a mile away."

There was also Mr Andersson, a sustainability consultant, whose job is to help big companies shrink their environmental footprint. He told us, in no uncertain terms, that within ten years every single company will employ someone in his role. "It's not a passing trend," he said. "It's the direction of travel."

And the most surprising one of all — Sofia, an AI ethics specialist. Her job is to make sure AI systems are fair, safe and don't accidentally make discriminatory decisions about real people. "AI is incredible," she said, "but it's built by humans, which means it can also be biased. My job is to keep it in check." She admitted that her exact role didn't exist a decade ago.

At the end of the session, our teacher asked us a brilliant question: "What job do YOU want to do?" The strange thing is — many of the jobs my classmates and I will eventually do probably haven't been invented yet. According to one of the speakers, around 65% of today's primary-school children will end up in jobs that don't currently exist. That's a wild statistic to get your head around.

What struck me most, though, wasn't the technology. It was the mindset shift. Most of these speakers had changed careers two or three times. None of them had climbed a single career ladder. They'd zigzagged, taken risks, picked up skills along the way, and weren't afraid to pivot when something stopped working. "A career isn't a straight line anymore," Mia told us. "It's more like a series of jumps."

When my dad finally got his head around all this, he just smiled and shrugged. "Sam, when I was your age, my dream was to land a stable office job and stay there until retirement." We both laughed. Times really do change. And honestly? I can't wait to find out what's next.

💬B2 vocabulary & collocations

The language you'll need to talk fluently about jobs, careers and the future. Pay attention to which words go together — that's where B2 students level up.

a profession / a vocation
a job that requires training; a job you feel called to do
"Teaching is a vocation, not just a profession."
to pursue a career (in)
to actively choose and follow a profession
"She wants to pursue a career in tech."
to land a job
to successfully get a job
"He landed his first job at a tech startup."
to climb the career ladder
to gradually move up to higher positions
"He's climbed the career ladder fast."
to pivot (in your career)
to change direction completely
"After 10 years in finance, she pivoted to design."
a career path
the route someone takes through their professional life
"My career path has been anything but linear."
to earn a six-figure salary
to earn 100,000 or more per year
"Top engineers can earn a six-figure salary."
to work remotely / fully remote
to do your job from anywhere, not the office
"More and more people work fully remotely now."
a hybrid role
a job split between office and home
"It's a hybrid role — three days in, two from home."
to upskill / reskill
to learn new skills / completely new ones
"He had to reskill to stay employable."
in-demand skills
skills employers really want right now
"AI literacy is one of the most in-demand skills."
a transferable skill
a skill useful in many different jobs
"Communication is the ultimate transferable skill."
a side hustle
a part-time job/business alongside your main one
"Her side hustle pays better than her day job."
the gig economy
work made up of short-term, freelance jobs
"The gig economy has changed everything."
to freelance / a freelancer
to work for yourself, on multiple short projects
"She freelances for several international clients."
a startup
a young, fast-growing company
"He works at a startup in Berlin."
to be passionate about (sth)
to deeply care about a topic or activity
"You can tell she's passionate about her work."
to make a living (doing sth)
to earn enough money from an activity to live on
"He makes a living posting cooking videos."
work-life balance
a healthy split between work and personal life
"Gen Z really care about work-life balance."
to burn out / burnout
to become exhausted from too much stress
"She quit because she was on the verge of burnout."
to automate (a task)
to make a machine do it
"Many boring tasks will be fully automated."
to disrupt an industry
to change it dramatically with new technology
"AI is disrupting almost every industry."
job security
the certainty that your job won't disappear
"Job security is harder to come by these days."
the direction of travel
the way things are clearly heading
"Sustainability isn't a trend — it's the direction of travel."
a mindset shift
a fundamental change in how people think
"Younger workers are causing a real mindset shift."

💡Idioms & expressions about work

B2-level idioms that pop up everywhere when people talk about jobs, careers and ambition. Learn them — they instantly make your English sound more natural.

to blow someone away
to amaze them completely
"That speech blew me away."
in a nutshell
in just a few words; summarised briefly
"In a nutshell, AI changes everything."
no two days are the same
every day brings something different
"In this job, no two days are the same."
to get your head around (sth)
to finally understand something difficult
"It took me a while to get my head around it."
to be a grind
to require hard, repetitive effort
"It looks glamorous, but honestly, it's a grind."
to smell something a mile away
to easily detect when something is fake
"Audiences smell fakeness a mile away."
to set someone straight
to correct someone's wrong ideas
"She set us straight about the reality of the job."
a passing trend
a fashion that won't last
"This isn't a passing trend — it's structural."
to keep something in check
to control it; to stop it going too far
"My job is to keep AI bias in check."
to be in your blood
to be a deep part of who you are
"Music is in his blood."
to follow in someone's footsteps
to do the same job as a relative
"She followed in her mother's footsteps."
to jump ship
to leave a company for a better one
"He jumped ship to a startup last year."
to call the shots
to be the one making the decisions
"As a freelancer, I call the shots."
to wear many hats
to do several different roles in a job
"In a startup, you wear many hats."
to learn the ropes
to learn the basics of a new job
"It took me six months to learn the ropes."
to think outside the box
to think creatively, beyond standard solutions
"Future jobs will reward people who think outside the box."
a foot in the door
a small first step into an industry
"That internship gave me a foot in the door."
to go the extra mile
to make significantly more effort than expected
"He always goes the extra mile for his team."
a dead-end job
a job with no chance of progress
"I quit — it was a complete dead-end job."
it pays the bills
it earns enough to live on (often unenthusiastically)
"It's not glamorous, but it pays the bills."
to make ends meet
to earn just enough money to survive
"He works two jobs to make ends meet."
a stepping stone
something that helps you move toward a bigger goal
"This role is a stepping stone to bigger things."

🎤Speaking practice

Speak for 1½–2 minutes per question. Use at least 3 target expressions from this lesson per answer. Practise with a partner — and remember to push the younger learner to use full sentences and the older students to stretch into idioms!

◆ QUESTION 01 · YOUR FAVOURITE NEW JOB
Look back at the Job Cards. Which three jobs do you find the most exciting, and why? Could you imagine yourself doing any of them?
Try to use: in a nutshell · no two days are the same · to be passionate about · to make a living · to land a job
◆ QUESTION 02 · YOUR DREAM JOB
Describe your dream job in detail. What would your typical day look like? What skills would you need?
Try to use: to pursue a career · in-demand skills · transferable skills · to wear many hats · work-life balance
◆ QUESTION 03 · OFFICE vs REMOTE
Would you prefer to work in a traditional office, fully remotely, or in a hybrid role? Defend your choice — and convince the rest of the class!
Try to use: to work remotely · a hybrid role · work-life balance · job security · in a nutshell
◆ QUESTION 04 · AI & YOUR FUTURE
Will AI take some jobs in the future? Which jobs will probably disappear, and which ones will always need humans? Be specific.
Try to use: to automate · to disrupt an industry · to keep something in check · the direction of travel · to upskill
◆ QUESTION 05 · CONTENT CREATOR LIFE
Could you make a living as a content creator on TikTok or YouTube? What would be the hardest part?
Try to use: to make a living · it's a grind · authenticity · to smell a mile away · burnout · to call the shots
◆ QUESTION 06 · CAREER PATHS
Some people climb a single career ladder for 30 years. Others zigzag and pivot many times. Which approach makes more sense to you, and why?
Try to use: to climb the career ladder · to pivot · a career path · to jump ship · a stepping stone · a mindset shift
◆ QUESTION 07 · INVENT A NEW PROFESSION
Invent a brand-new job that doesn't yet exist. Give it a name. Explain what the person does, what skills they need, and why your invented job will exist in 10 years.
Try to use: in a nutshell · in-demand skills · to think outside the box · the direction of travel · a mindset shift

📝Grammar: Future Forms & Future Perfect

A B2 essential — using the right future form to talk about predictions, plans and what will be true by a certain time. Perfect topic, since "future of work" is full of forecasts!

🚀 The B2 future toolkit

  • will + infinitive → predictions based on opinion / general beliefs. I think AI will change everything.
  • be going to + infinitive → predictions based on present evidence; intentions. Look at this trend — drone pilots are going to be everywhere.
  • present continuous → fixed plans/arrangements (often with a time). I'm meeting a careers advisor on Friday.
  • future perfect (will have + past participle) → something completed by a future time. By 2035, AI will have replaced many routine jobs.
  • future continuous (will be + -ing) → something in progress at a future moment. This time next year, I'll be working from a beach in Bali.
Evidence now → going to | Opinion / prediction → will | Plan → present continuous
By + time + future perfect → "By 2030, robots will have taken over."
