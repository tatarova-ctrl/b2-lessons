STEP 05 · GRAMMAR FOCUS📝 Modal verbs

🧘 Obligation, advice & deduction

  • Obligation: must (strong, often personal) / have to (external rule) → I have to take this twice a day.
  • Prohibition: mustn't / can't → You mustn't eat before the blood test.
  • No obligation: don't have to / needn't → You don't have to come.
  • Advice: should / ought to / had better → You should sleep more. You'd better see a doctor.
  • Deduction: must (sure it's true) / can't (sure it's false) / might, could (possible) → He can't be ill — he's at the gym!
strong logical guess → MUST · impossible → CAN'T
advice → SHOULD / OUGHT TO / HAD BETTER

STEP 05 · GRAMMAR FOCUS📝 Relative clauses

✈️ Joining ideas with who / which / that / where / whose

  • who = people · which = things · that = people/things (defining only) · where = places · whose = possession.
  • Defining clauses give essential information — no commas: The guide who helped us was great.
  • Non-defining clauses add extra info — use commas, and you cannot use that: Rome, which I love, is busy.
  • Use when for times: Summer is the season when most people travel.
extra info + commas → who / which (NOT that)
place → WHERE · time → WHEN · possession → WHOSE

STEP 05 · GRAMMAR FOCUS📝 Conditionals (0 · 1 · 2)

🌍 Three conditionals for talking about the planet

  • Zero (facts/general truths): If + present, present → If you cut down forests, animals lose their homes.
  • First (real future possibility): If + present, will + verb → If we recycle more, we will reduce waste.
  • Second (unreal / hypothetical now): If + past, would + verb → If governments invested in solar, emissions would fall.
  • In the second conditional, use were for all persons: If I were president…
fact → present + present · real future → present + WILL
imaginary now → past + WOULD

STEP 05 · GRAMMAR FOCUS📝 Gerunds & infinitives

💰 -ing or to + verb?

  • Verb + gerund (-ing): enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, keep, consider, suggest, give up, can't help → I avoid spending too much.
  • Verb + infinitive (to): decide, want, hope, manage, afford, agree, refuse, plan, learn, offer, promise → We decided to save.
  • After a preposition → gerund: good at budgeting, interested in investing, instead of spending.
  • To express purpose → infinitive: I work extra hours to save money.
avoid / enjoy / keep / good at … → -ING
decide / want / manage / afford … → TO + verb
