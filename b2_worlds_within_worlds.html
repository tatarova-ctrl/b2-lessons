STEP 05 · GRAMMAR FOCUS📝 Comparatives & superlatives (advanced)

🏙️ Beyond -er and -est

  • Equality: as + adjective + as → The suburbs are as busy as the centre. Negative: not as / not so … as.
  • Make a comparison stronger: much, far, a lot, considerably + comparative → It's far cheaper here.
  • Make a comparison smaller: a bit, slightly, a little + comparative → It's slightly noisier.
  • Emphasise a superlative: by far the … → It's by far the biggest city.
  • Two changes that go together: the + comparative …, the + comparative … → The bigger the city, the higher the rent.
FAR / MUCH / A LOT + comparative · A BIT / SLIGHTLY + comparative
THE more …, THE more … (parallel change)

Complete each sentence with the correct form.

STEP 05 · GRAMMAR FOCUS📝 Modals of past deduction

🐘 Guessing about what happened

  • Use these to make a logical guess about the past.
  • Form: modal + have + past participle.
  • Sure it's true: must have → The grass is flat — a deer must have slept here.
  • Sure it's false: can't have / couldn't have → It can't have been a tiger — they don't live here.
  • Possible: might have, could have, may have → The bird might have flown south already.
SURE TRUE → must have + p.p. · SURE FALSE → can't have + p.p.
MAYBE → might / could / may have + p.p.

STEP 05 · GRAMMAR FOCUS📝 so / such / too / enough

🎉 Adding emphasis and limits

  • so + adjective/adverb → The food was so good!
  • such + (a/an) + (adjective) + noun → It was such a fun day. It was such fun!
  • too + adjective = more than is wanted: The music is too loud.
  • adjective + enough = the right amount: She's old enough to stay up.
  • enough + noun = the right quantity: We don't have enough chairs.
  • Result clause: so/such … (that)…; purpose with too/enough: too/enough … to + verb.
so + ADJ · such + (a) (ADJ) NOUN · too + ADJ (to…) · ADJ + enough (to…)

STEP 05 · GRAMMAR FOCUS📝 -ed vs -ing adjectives

💭 How I feel vs what causes the feeling

  • -ed adjectives describe how a person feels: I'm bored. She felt excited. He looked surprised.
  • -ing adjectives describe the thing that causes the feeling: The lesson is boring. The news is exciting. The film was surprising.
  • Test: a person is interested, a book is interesting. A person is tired, a journey is tiring.
  • Common pairs: bored/boring, tired/tiring, excited/exciting, interested/interesting, surprised/surprising, shocked/shocking, amazed/amazing, frightened/frightening, confused/confusing, embarrassed/embarrassing.
PERSON feels → -ED · THING causes the feeling → -ING
