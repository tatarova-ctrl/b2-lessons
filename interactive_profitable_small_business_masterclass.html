📖 Reading Article

How One Small Coffee Brand Became a National Success

When Daniel opened his first coffee stand, he had less than €2,000 in savings and almost no business experience. He was working a full-time office job during the day and selling coffee from a tiny mobile cart early in the mornings. Most people around him believed the business would fail quickly because the city already had dozens of established coffee shops.

However, Daniel noticed something important: many large coffee chains felt impersonal and corporate. Customers wanted something more authentic. Instead of competing on price alone, he focused on building a memorable brand identity and excellent customer relationships.

His strategy was surprisingly simple. He learned customers’ names, remembered their regular orders and created a strong social media presence using short behind-the-scenes videos. Slowly, people started recommending his business through word of mouth. This created free marketing and built trust much faster than traditional advertising.

Daniel also controlled his overhead costs carefully. Rather than renting an expensive café immediately, he operated from the mobile cart for almost two years. This allowed him to maintain healthy cash flow while testing the market safely.

Another smart decision was targeting a clear niche market. Instead of trying to sell everything, he focused only on premium organic coffee and environmentally friendly packaging. Customers appreciated the consistency and clear company values.

As profits increased, Daniel reinvested money into branding, packaging and customer experience. Today, his company operates in several cities and employs more than 40 people. Despite the growth, he believes the key to profitability is still the same: solve real customer problems, communicate clearly and build long-term trust.

💬 Advanced Business Vocabulary

Study the vocabulary carefully. Try to use the expressions naturally during speaking activities.
profit margin
The percentage of money earned after costs.
“Luxury brands usually have high profit margins.”
startup capital
Money used to launch a business.
“Many entrepreneurs struggle to raise startup capital.”
scalable
A business that can grow easily.
“Software companies are highly scalable.”
customer retention
Keeping customers loyal over time.
“Customer retention is cheaper than acquisition.”
brand awareness
How well people know a company.
“Social media increased brand awareness rapidly.”
competitive advantage
A feature that makes a business stronger than rivals.
“Fast delivery became their competitive advantage.”
market research
Studying customer needs and trends.
“Good market research reduces business risk.”
revenue stream
A source of company income.
“Subscriptions created a stable revenue stream.”
target demographic
The exact group of customers a business wants.
“Teenagers are their target demographic.”
burnout
Extreme stress and exhaustion from work.
“Many founders experience burnout.”
networking
Building professional relationships.
“Networking opened new business opportunities.”
pricing strategy
The method used to set prices.
“Their pricing strategy targeted premium buyers.”

Second Conditional: imaginary or hypothetical situations.

Zero Conditional: general truths and facts.

If the company had invested in marketing earlier, sales would have grown faster.

Third Conditional: past hypothetical situations.
