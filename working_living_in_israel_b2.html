📖Reading

Read Sophie's account of relocating from London to Tel Aviv for a tech job — and what surprised her most.

"Nobody Asks for Permission Here"

★ Sophie Hart, 31 · Software Engineer · London → Tel Aviv · 3 years

I landed in Tel Aviv with two suitcases, a job offer from a cybersecurity startup, and absolutely no idea what I was getting into. Within a week, a 24-year-old colleague had challenged my code in a company-wide meeting. In London, that would have been career suicide. Here, everyone nodded approvingly. Welcome to Israel's flat hierarchy culture — where chutzpah, the Hebrew word for bold audacity, is not just tolerated; it's expected.

Israel calls itself the Startup Nation, and the numbers back it up. With just 9.7 million people, the country has more startups per capita than any nation on Earth — roughly one startup for every 1,400 people. Over 300 multinational companies — Apple, Google, Intel, Amazon — have set up R&D centres here. My office was three blocks from Google Israel. The energy is electric. Meetings are loud, ideas fly fast, and nobody waits for permission before trying something new.

The lifestyle took more getting used to. Tel Aviv is expensive — 20–30% pricier than most Western European cities — and the bureaucracy can drive you up the wall. On the other hand, the Mediterranean beach is twenty minutes from my office, the food is extraordinary, and the social life never stops. Israelis are extraordinarily warm once you earn their trust — though their directness can feel rude at first if you're British.

Living here means accepting complexity. Security sirens are part of daily life. Yet most people get on with it with remarkable resilience. As my colleague Noa told me: "This is home. You don't leave home just because it's complicated." Three years in, I'm not leaving either.

📚Vocabulary

Study these key terms related to work culture, expat life, and Israel's economy.

chutzpah
Hebrew/Yiddish: bold audacity or nerve; the confidence to do something others wouldn't dare.
It took real chutzpah to question the CEO's strategy in public.
flat hierarchy
An organisational structure with few management levels, where junior staff can challenge seniors.
Israeli startups are known for their flat hierarchy and open debate.
expat
Short for expatriate; a person living and working outside their home country.
Tel Aviv has a growing expat community attracted by tech jobs.
aliyah
Hebrew for "going up"; the immigration of Jewish people to Israel.
Thousands make aliyah each year to settle in Israel permanently.
unicorn
A privately held startup company valued at over one billion dollars.
Israel has produced nearly 100 unicorns, including Waze and Mobileye.
R&D centre
Research and Development facility where companies create new technologies or products.
Apple and Google both have major R&D centres in Tel Aviv.
resilience
The ability to recover quickly and continue despite difficulty or adversity.
Israelis demonstrate remarkable resilience in the face of security challenges.
bureaucracy
Complex official systems with many rules, forms, and slow processes.
Expats often struggle with Israel's bureaucracy when opening bank accounts.
per capita
Per person; calculated by dividing a total figure by the population.
Israel has more startups per capita than any other country.
cybersecurity
The practice of protecting systems, networks, and data from digital attacks.
Israel is a world leader in cybersecurity — one in three unicorns globally is Israeli.

🎯Common Collocations

Learn word combinations used when discussing expat life, work culture, and technology.

💼 Work culture collocations

  • flat hierarchy = few management levels; junior staff challenge seniors freely. Israel's flat hierarchy culture encourages open debate at all levels.
  • work-life balance = equilibrium between job and personal time. Tech workers in Tel Aviv often sacrifice work-life balance for career growth.
  • fast-paced environment = a setting where things move quickly. Startups offer a fast-paced environment that suits ambitious professionals.
  • cross-cultural teamwork = collaboration between people of different backgrounds. Israeli offices require strong cross-cultural teamwork skills.

🌍 Expat life collocations

  • cost of living = the amount needed to cover basic expenses. The cost of living in Tel Aviv is among the highest in the world.
  • culture shock = the disorientation felt when experiencing an unfamiliar culture. Sophie experienced culture shock in her first Israeli workplace meeting.
  • settle in = to become comfortable in a new place. It took her six months to settle in and feel at home.
  • language barrier = difficulty communicating due to different languages. English is widely spoken in Israeli tech, so the language barrier is low.

🚀 Tech and innovation collocations

  • raise funding = to gather investment capital for a startup. Israeli startups raised $12.2 billion in funding in 2024.
  • set up an R&D centre = to establish a research facility. Over 300 multinationals have set up R&D centres in Israel.
  • venture capital = investment in high-risk startups. Tel Aviv attracts twice as much venture capital as the US per capita.
  • disruptive technology = innovation that transforms existing industries. Israeli companies are known for producing disruptive technology.

⚙️Modal Verbs

Complete sentences using modal verbs to express ability, obligation, advice, and possibility about expat life in Israel.

⚙️ Modal Verbs — Key Uses

  • can / could — ability or possibility. You can work in Israel if you have a valid work permit.
  • must / have to — strong obligation. Expats must register with local authorities within 90 days.
  • should / ought to — advice or recommendation. You should learn some basic Hebrew before moving.
  • might / may — weak possibility or uncertainty. The culture shock might surprise you in the first weeks.
  • needn't / don't have to — no obligation. You needn't speak Hebrew — English is widely used in tech.

🔗Discourse Markers

Complete sentences using discourse markers to link ideas, contrast information, and structure arguments about life in Israel.

🔗 Discourse Markers — Key Types

  • Contrast: however, on the other hand, nevertheless, despite, even though. Tel Aviv is expensive; however, salaries are competitive.
  • Addition: furthermore, moreover, in addition, what is more. Israel has a booming tech sector; furthermore, it leads in cybersecurity.
  • Result: therefore, as a result, consequently, thus. English is widely spoken; therefore, the language barrier is low.
  • Exemplification: for instance, for example, such as, in particular. Many multinationals, such as Apple and Google, have offices there.
  • Concession: admittedly, granted, of course. Admittedly, the bureaucracy is frustrating, but most expats adapt.
