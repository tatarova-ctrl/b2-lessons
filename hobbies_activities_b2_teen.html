📖Reading

Read about Mia — a 17-year-old who has turned her hobbies into something much bigger than she expected.

The Side Hustle Generation

★ Mia Kowalski, 17 · Warsaw · Content Creator, Gamer, Athlete · 2025

When Mia was fourteen, she started editing short gaming videos and uploading them for fun. Three years later, she has amassed 47,000 followers across YouTube and TikTok, signed a small brand deal with a gaming headset company, and is seriously considering a career in digital media. None of this was planned. "I just loved editing," she says. "I never thought it would take off the way it did." Her parents, initially sceptical, have come around. Her school, less so.

"The funny thing," Mia explains, "is that the skills I've developed through content creation are the ones that actually matter. I've taught myself video editing, graphic design, scriptwriting, and basic marketing — all without a single lesson." She also co-runs an amateur esports team that competes in regional tournaments, training three times a week. The team has been going for eighteen months and recently qualified for a national competition. "People assume gaming is just mindless entertainment. But organising a team, managing schedules, and preparing strategies — it's not that different from running a business."

Beyond the screen, Mia swims competitively and has been playing the violin since she was seven. She gave up competitive swimming at sixteen — it was consuming too much time — but still goes for long morning swims three times a week for mental clarity. "When I'm in the water," she says, "my brain completely switches off. I've solved more problems swimming than sitting at a desk." The violin she never abandoned. Despite having a packed schedule, she practises for thirty minutes every night — not because anyone makes her, but because she finds it genuinely meditative.

The broader picture Mia represents is significant. A 2024 study found that teenagers who pursue creative hobbies alongside academic work show higher levels of resilience, stronger problem-solving ability, and better emotional regulation than those who don't. The challenge isn't convincing teenagers to have hobbies — they already have them. The challenge is convincing the adults around them that not all learning looks like school.

🎯Common Collocations

Learn these natural word combinations used when talking about hobbies, ambition, and teen life.

🎮 Hobbies and passions

  • pursue a hobby = to actively follow and develop an interest. Teenagers who pursue creative hobbies develop stronger problem-solving skills.
  • develop a skill = to improve an ability through practice and effort. She developed her editing skills entirely through self-teaching.
  • dedicate time to = to choose to spend time on something important. Despite her busy schedule, she dedicates 30 minutes a day to violin.
  • nurture a talent = to care for and develop a natural ability. Schools should do more to nurture talent outside of academic subjects.

💡 Success and ambition

  • turn a passion into a career = to make your hobby your profession. More teenagers are turning their passions into careers through social media.
  • build an audience = to grow a group of followers or viewers over time. Building an audience takes months of consistent effort and original content.
  • make a name for yourself = to become known and respected in a field. She's making a name for herself in the esports community at just 17.
  • break into an industry = to successfully enter a competitive field. Digital media is a hard industry to break into without a strong portfolio.

🧠 Wellbeing and balance

  • switch off = to stop thinking about problems and relax completely. Swimming is the only time she truly switches off from everything.
  • mental wellbeing = the overall health of your mind, emotions, and thoughts. Regular physical activity is proven to boost mental wellbeing in teenagers.
  • strike a balance = to find the right amount of two competing things. She has managed to strike a balance between school, sport, and content creation.
  • take its toll = to gradually cause damage or exhaustion. Too many activities can take their toll on your energy and performance.

⏱️Perfect Tenses

Complete the exercises using the correct perfect tense form.

⏱️ Perfect Tenses — Key Uses

  • Present Perfect Simple: life experience or recent past with present result. Mia has amassed 47,000 followers since she started posting.
  • Present Perfect Continuous: ongoing action from past to now — emphasises duration. She has been playing violin since she was seven. / The team has been competing for 18 months.
  • Past Perfect: something that happened before another past event. By the time she was 17, she had already signed a brand deal.
  • Key signals: since, for, already, yet, just, ever, never, by the time, before.

⚖️Concession & Contrast

Concession structures allow you to acknowledge one side while arguing another. They make your speaking and writing much more sophisticated.

⚖️ Concession & Contrast Structures

  • Although / Even though + clause: Although gaming has a bad reputation, it develops real teamwork skills.
  • Despite / In spite of + noun/gerund: Despite having a packed schedule, she practises violin every night.
  • However / Nevertheless / Even so: used to introduce a contrasting point. Gaming can be addictive. Nevertheless, the evidence for its benefits is strong.
  • Admittedly: acknowledges a weakness in your argument before moving on. Admittedly, not every teenager will turn their hobby into a career.
  • Whereas / While: compares two contrasting ideas. While some see gaming as a waste of time, others view it as a valuable skill.
