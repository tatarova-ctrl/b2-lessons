🥗 Food & Healthy Eating

What healthy eating really looks like. Highlighted phrases are everyday expressions.

What Your Grandmother Was Right About

★ Elena Marchetti · Food doctor · 20 years working with patients

Every week, the news tells us something new about food. Coffee is good. Coffee is bad. Eggs kill you. Eggs save you. If you try to follow all of it, you will feel crazy. After twenty years working with patients, I can tell you the truth is simple and boring: your grandmother was right. Eat real food. Eat mostly plants. Don't eat too much. Stop when you feel full. Everything else is noise.

Your body needs three main things: carbohydrates, protein and fat. All three are important. Carbs give you energy — rice, bread, oats, pasta. Protein builds your body — fish, eggs, beans, chicken. Healthy fats protect your heart — olive oil, nuts, oily fish. Please don't try to cut out one whole group. Your body needs all three.

“A good plate is not a punishment. It is what your body has been asking for, quietly, for years.”

There are two quiet heroes: fibre and vitamins. Fibre comes from vegetables, fruit, oats and beans. It helps your stomach work. It makes you feel full. Most adults eat only half of what they need. Vitamins come mostly from colourful plants. A plate with three colours is a good plate.

The real problem in modern food is not sugar or fat. It is ultra-processed food: crisps, sweet drinks, ready meals in plastic. This food is made to taste good so you eat more. It also takes the place of real food. If most of your meals come in a plastic pack, your body slowly gets tired and ill.

Portion size is important too. Restaurant meals today are much bigger than 40 years ago. Try to eat from a smaller plate for a month. You will feel just as happy. Also, eat slowly. Your brain needs 20 minutes to feel full. Eating fast is an easy way to eat too much.

Water is also important. Many adults do not drink enough water. Tea and coffee are fine. But start your day with one glass of water. Sometimes we think we are hungry, but we are just thirsty.

There is no perfect diet in the world. Mediterranean food, plant food, home food from many cultures — all of them work. The important thing is real food, cooked at home, eaten slowly, with people you like. That is not a diet. That is a good life.

If you take one idea, take this. Don't give up a food forever. Don't go on a hard diet for three weeks. Small changes, for many years, do more than any short diet. Fill up on vegetables. Cut down on ultra-processed food. Eat at home when you can. Boring? Yes. Life-changing? Also yes.

🚴 Movement & Sleep

The small daily habits that change how your body and mind feel.

The Boring Habits That Change Everything

★ Adam Kowalski · Health coach · 15 years working with tired people

Many people come to me and want a special exercise plan. After 15 years, I can tell you three boring things change your body and your mood more than any plan: walking, sleeping, and getting some morning sun. Do these three well, and you are half way to healthy. No gym needed. Ignore them, and no plan will help you.

Let's start with movement. Today, many adults sit for nine hours every day. Our body was not made for this. You don't need to work out for hours. You just need to move a little every day. Ten thousand steps is a nice number, but seven thousand is enough for most people. The trick is to start small. If you now walk 2,000 steps a day, try 3,000 next week. Not 10,000. Small wins are better than big fails.

“The body is not broken by big things. It is broken by nine hours of sitting, two nights of bad sleep, and forgetting how to move.”

Then there is strength. From the age of 35, our muscles get smaller if we don't use them. Two short strength sessions a week are enough. Push-ups. Squats. Carrying shopping up the stairs. All of this counts. You don't need a gym. You need a habit.

Now, sleep. Most adults need seven to nine hours. If you sleep less than six hours often, everything gets worse: mood, hunger, focus. Sleep is not a luxury. It is medicine. But many people stay up late looking at their phone. Two simple changes fix most sleep problems. First, go to bed and wake up at the same time every day. Second, put your phone down 30 minutes before sleep.

Small sleep habits are called sleep hygiene. Keep your bedroom cool and dark. Use your bed only for sleeping. Don't eat heavy food three hours before bed. If you sleep badly, don't drink coffee after 2 p.m. Alcohol makes you fall asleep fast, but you sleep badly after. If you often wake up at 3 a.m., wine is often the reason.

Now, morning light. Ten minutes outside in the morning helps your body all day. It doesn't have to be sunny. Outside light is much stronger than indoor light. Add a short walk and you have a free way to feel better. In dark winter mornings, a special lamp can help.

There is also the feeling side. Loneliness is now as bad for your body as smoking. One short walk with a friend is very powerful. So is a phone call to your mother. Being healthy is not only food and sport. It is also the people around you.

If you take one idea, take this. Don't try to change everything at once. Pick one habit — a daily walk, a fixed bedtime, ten minutes of morning sun. Keep going for four weeks. Then add the next one. In one year, you will have five new habits and a very different body. It will not feel big. That is why it works.

🚭 Stopping Bad Habits

Why willpower is not enough — and what really helps.

The Habit You Didn't Choose

★ Dr Sofia Torres · Habit doctor · Helps people stop what they wish they hadn't started

Every bad habit started as a small answer to a problem. You took your phone because you were bored. You opened the wine because you were tired. You ordered pizza because you didn't want to cook. In that moment, it fixed something small. But you did it a thousand times, and now it is part of your day. This is the strange truth about bad habits: you didn't really choose them. They chose you.

The most important idea in modern habit science is the habit loop. It has three parts: a trigger, a routine, and a reward. Something happens (you feel tired). You do something (you scroll your phone). You feel a little better for a short time. Your brain learns this pattern. To stop a bad habit, you must understand the loop. Don't just fight yourself.

“Willpower is not a personality. It is a tired muscle by 7 p.m. If your plan needs a lot of willpower, your plan will fail.”

This is why “trying harder” rarely works. You do well for four days. Then, on a bad evening, you give up. The best trick is to change the world around you, not fight yourself. If you want to stop looking at your phone at night, put it in another room. If you want to cut down on wine, don't buy three bottles. If you want to eat less sugar, don't keep it at home. If the bad thing is not near you, it is much easier.

The four habits many people want to stop are the same in every country. Smoking. Drinking too much. Junk food. And now, a new one: too much phone. Alcohol slowly damages your body. Junk food makes you ill after many years. Too much phone hurts sleep, mood and real friendships. Every one of these looks small day by day. But together they change your whole life.

The best way depends on the habit. Some are best to cut out completely. Ex-smokers say zero cigarettes is easier than “just one”. Other habits are better to reduce slowly: from three coffees to two, from four beers a week to two. The wrong plan is the one that makes you feel sad every day.

There is also the question of why. Bad habits are not just bad choices. They are usually small answers to real problems: you feel lonely, bored, stressed, unhappy at work. If you don't deal with the real problem, the habit comes back in another shape. Some people stop smoking and start eating too much. The habit changes. The empty feeling stays.

There is also the power of identity. Don't say “I'm trying to stop smoking.” Say “I don't smoke.” It sounds small. It works. Your brain believes the story you tell about yourself.

There will be bad days. One bad night after four good weeks is not a failure. It is just one day. The people who really change are not perfect. They just start again on Tuesday morning.

If you take one idea, take this. Don't use willpower. Change your world. Understand the loop. Deal with the real feeling under the habit. Change the story you tell yourself. When you fall, be kind to yourself and start again. That is how habits really die: slowly, not perfectly, over many months. Not on a big new Monday.

🌱 Building a Healthier Life

How to really build habits that last — without hating your life.

Small Steps, Long Roads

★ Dr James Okoro · Family doctor · 20 years watching people change (or not)

For 20 years, I have watched my patients try to change their lives every January. Big plans. New gym cards. New diet books. By March, most of them are back to their old life. It is not because they are weak. It is because they believe the wrong story: that being healthy is a big hero project. In real life, health is much slower and quieter. It is a long conversation between you, your home, and the people around you.

The most important idea in habit science is that motivation dies. It always does. Nobody feels ready every day. If your plan needs you to feel motivated on a rainy Wednesday in February, your plan will fail. The people who really build healthy lives don't need motivation. They make the good choice the easy choice. They make the bad choice a little harder.

“You will not feel like it most mornings. The people who win are just the ones who go and do it anyway, in a small size that is not a hero act.”

Let's start with habit stacking. Add a new small habit to a habit you already have. After your morning coffee, do 10 push-ups. Before you check your phone in bed, drink a glass of water. After lunch, walk for five minutes. The old habit becomes the trigger for the new one. Your brain learns the pattern.

Then there is the world around you. Make good habits easy. Put your running shoes by the door. Fill the fruit bowl. Move the kettle far from the biscuits. Make bad habits hard. Delete the shopping app. Leave your phone in another room. Every small thing helps in the long run.

The second big idea is to start small. Very small. Instead of “I'll run four times a week,” try “I'll put on my shoes and walk to the corner every morning.” Some days you will go further. Some days you won't. But you will never miss a day. Never missing is more important than sometimes doing a lot. In one year, that very small habit becomes a real one.

Then there is the power of identity. If you want to be a healthy person, act like one in small ways today. Cook one more meal at home this week. Walk instead of taking the bus twice. Each small action is a small vote for the person you want to be. Your brain slowly changes the story: “I'm a person who cooks.”

Your friends matter more than you think. If your five closest friends walk every day, you will walk. If they order takeaway every night, so will you. Make friends with people whose habits you like. Join a walking group, a swimming class, or a cooking club. Being healthy is easier when it is normal in your group.

Now, the hard part: failure. You will miss weeks. You will fall. Life will happen — illness, work, sad times. The people who really build healthy lives are not the ones who never fail. They are the ones who start again quickly, without drama, without “I ruined everything.”

Finally, be patient. Real change takes years, not weeks. In one month, nothing looks different. In one year, small changes will slowly change your energy, sleep, mood, and body. In five years, you will not recognise the tired person you were before. Not because you did something big. Because you did small things again and again.

If you take one idea, take this. Don't build a big new life. Build a slightly better next hour. Then do it again, quietly, for a long time. That is how real health is built — not on Monday, and not by magic. On Tuesday. And Wednesday. And the Tuesday after.
