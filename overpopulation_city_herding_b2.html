📖Reading

Read this opinion piece challenging the idea that the world is dangerously overpopulated.

The Empty Planet Nobody Told You About

Opinion | Dr. Carla Reeves, Urban Geographer | Global Cities Review, 2025

Here is a fact that stops people cold: the entire human population of Earth — all 8.2 billion of us — could fit inside the state of Texas, with each person having roughly the space of a small apartment. The rest of the planet would be completely empty. Not crowded. Empty. So why does everyone talk about overpopulation as though we are running out of room?

The answer is not population. It's geography. Cities occupy just 3% of Earth's land area, yet over 55% of the world's population lives in them. Tokyo alone holds 37 million people. Mumbai fits 21 million into a sliver of coastline. Meanwhile, vast regions of Canada, Siberia, central Australia, and the American plains sit virtually empty. The problem is not that there are too many people. The problem is that almost everyone is choosing — or being forced — to live in the same few places.

Call it city herding: the gravitational pull of economic opportunity, education, healthcare, and social life that draws people into dense urban cores, regardless of the crushing cost and overcrowding. A young engineer in rural Nigeria isn't moving to Lagos because she enjoys traffic jams. She's moving because Lagos has the jobs, the hospitals, and the internet connections that her village simply doesn't. This is not a population crisis. It is an infrastructure crisis disguised as one.

What we need is not fewer people. What we need is better distribution — investment in rural infrastructure, remote work opportunities, and decentralised services that make it viable to spread out. The planet has the space. The question is whether we have the political will to use it.

📚Vocabulary

Study these key terms related to population, urbanisation, and geography.

overpopulation
The state of having more people in an area than available resources can sustainably support.
Critics argue overpopulation is a myth — the issue is distribution, not numbers.
urbanisation
The process by which increasing numbers of people move from rural areas to cities.
Rapid urbanisation has transformed cities like Lagos and Mumbai beyond recognition.
population density
The number of people living per unit of area, such as per square kilometre.
Tokyo has one of the highest population densities on Earth.
infrastructure
Basic systems and structures needed for a society to function (roads, hospitals, internet).
Poor rural infrastructure drives people toward cities for basic services.
decentralisation
The process of moving power, services, or population away from a central location.
Decentralisation of hospitals and schools could slow urban migration.
migration
The movement of people from one place to another, especially in large numbers.
Rural-to-urban migration is accelerating across Asia and Africa.
slum
An overcrowded, poor urban area with inadequate housing and services.
Over 1.1 billion people currently live in urban slums worldwide.
habitable
Suitable for people to live in; fit for human habitation.
Only about 30% of Earth's surface is habitable land.
carrying capacity
The maximum population an environment can sustain without being degraded.
Some scientists argue Earth's carrying capacity is being approached.
megacity
An urban area with a population exceeding ten million people.
There are now 34 megacities globally, all facing extreme density challenges.

🎯Common Collocations

Learn word combinations used in discussions about population, cities, and resources.

🌆 Population and cities

  • urban sprawl = uncontrolled spread of a city into surrounding areas. Urban sprawl is consuming farmland on the outskirts of every major city.
  • population boom = a rapid and large increase in population. The post-war population boom transformed housing demand globally.
  • population distribution = how people are spread across an area. Uneven population distribution causes resource shortages in cities.
  • rural depopulation = declining number of people living in countryside. Rural depopulation is emptying entire villages in southern Italy.

🏗️ Resources and space

  • scarce resources = limited supplies that are difficult to obtain. Overcrowded cities put enormous pressure on scarce resources.
  • land use = the way a piece of land is used or managed. Efficient land use is essential for feeding a growing population.
  • finite resources = resources that will eventually run out. Fossil fuels are finite resources we must use responsibly.
  • standard of living = the level of comfort and material wellbeing in a place. Megacity slums have a far lower standard of living than city centres.

💬 Debate and argument collocations

  • challenge the notion = to question or dispute a widely accepted idea. The author challenges the notion that overpopulation is inevitable.
  • make the case for = to argue in favour of something. She makes a strong case for decentralised development.
  • widely held belief = something many people accept as true. The overpopulation crisis is a widely held belief, but is it accurate?
  • conventional wisdom = what most people generally accept as common sense. The author turns conventional wisdom on overpopulation upside down.

🔍Cleft Sentences

Cleft sentences put special emphasis on one part of a sentence. Complete the exercises below.

🔍 Cleft Sentence Structures

  • It is/was... that/who: emphasises a subject, object, or time. It is city herding that causes overcrowding, not the number of people.
  • What... is/was: emphasises the action or situation. What we need is better infrastructure, not fewer people.
  • The reason... is that: emphasises cause. The reason people move to cities is that rural areas lack basic services.
  • All... is: stresses the only thing needed. All we need is better distribution of resources.

💬Hedging Language

Hedging makes arguments sound more academic and balanced. Complete the exercises using hedging expressions.

💬 Common Hedging Expressions

  • It could be argued that... — introduces a viewpoint cautiously. It could be argued that overcrowding is a choice, not a necessity.
  • It seems / appears that... — suggests without certainty. It appears that most people prefer city life despite the costs.
  • tend to / are likely to — describes general patterns. People who move to cities tend to earn higher wages.
  • may / might / could — expresses possibility. Decentralisation might reduce pressure on megacities significantly.
  • to some extent / in many cases — limits generalisations. To some extent, overpopulation is a problem of perception, not reality.
