The four zones — where do you spend your time?

RING 1
Comfort zone

Feels safe. Familiar routines. Nothing new happens here.

RING 2

Self-doubt, excuses. "What will people think?" "I might fail."

New goals. New purpose. You become a different version of yourself.

📖Reading text

A young professional shares the story of how saying yes to one uncomfortable thing changed everything. Target language is in gold bold.

"The Year I Stopped Hiding"

★ Lena, 29 · marketing manager · Lisbon

Three years ago, I was someone who played it safe. Same office, same friends, same routine, same small life. I always told myself I was happy — and to be fair, I wasn't unhappy. But I had this quiet feeling that I was, well, slowly disappearing. Then a friend invited me to do a beginners' improv class. Improv. In a foreign language. In front of strangers. My initial reaction was a flat "absolutely not." But for some reason, I said yes anyway. And honestly? That single decision changed my life.

The first class was, in a word, mortifying. I felt like a complete fish out of water. Everyone seemed faster, funnier, more confident. I forgot my own name during one exercise. I went home and lay on the floor for an hour wondering why I'd done that to myself. The temptation to never go back was huge. But — and here's the thing — I went back. And the second time was a tiny bit easier. Then the third was easier still.

That's when I learned my first real lesson about stepping out of your comfort zone: it doesn't get less scary. You get braver. There's a big difference.

"Life begins at the end of your comfort zone." — Neale Donald Walsch

Once I'd broken the seal with improv, something strange happened. Other doors started feeling more open. I signed up for a public speaking workshop — the kind where they make you stand up and tell a story to thirty strangers. I took the plunge and started a side project, designing posters, even though I had no formal training. I went on a solo trip to Japan, where I knew nobody. Each one of these things terrified me a little less than the one before. It was as if my confidence muscle was getting stronger every time I used it.

Of course, it wasn't all smooth sailing. I fell flat on my face more times than I'd like to admit. The poster business was a slow start — for the first six months I made about €40 in total. The Japan trip had a moment, somewhere in Osaka, where I burst into tears in a tiny restaurant because I couldn't read the menu and I missed my mum. But here's the thing nobody tells you: the bad moments don't last. They become stories. They become "remember when…" jokes. And every single one of them taught me something I couldn't have learned by staying home.

What surprised me most was how much my everyday life started changing too. I started speaking up more in meetings. I asked for a pay rise — and got it. I ended a friendship that had been quietly draining me for years. I tried foods I'd dismissed as "not for me" and discovered I actually love half of them. I think this is what people mean when they talk about personal growth — it's not really about doing huge dramatic things. It's about slowly shifting what you believe is possible for you.

I'm not going to pretend it's all sunshine and rainbows. Stepping out of your comfort zone takes guts. There are days when I want to retreat back into the familiar. There are moments when the fear of looking stupid is overwhelming. But I've learned to recognise that fear for what it is — a sign that I'm doing something worth doing. As one of my improv teachers once said: "If you're not slightly uncomfortable, you're not learning."

If I could give my 26-year-old self one piece of advice, it would be this: stop waiting until you "feel ready". You won't. Ready is a feeling that arrives only after you've already started. Pick the smallest scary thing you can think of. Sign up for the class. Send the message. Just dive in. The version of you on the other side will thank you.

🎤Speaking practice — your stories & your views

Speak 1½–2 minutes per question. Use at least 3 target expressions per answer. This is a chance to be honest and personal — that's where the best speaking practice happens.

◆ QUESTION 01 · YOUR COMFORT ZONE
How would you describe your own comfort zone? In which areas of your life are you brave, and in which are you a real "stay-at-home"?
Try to use: comfort zone · play it safe · push my limits · self-doubt · everyday life · gain confidence
◆ QUESTION 02 · A TIME YOU TOOK THE PLUNGE
Tell us about a moment in your life when you took a real risk. How did it feel before, during, and after?
Try to use: to take the plunge · a leap of faith · fish out of water · to grow as a person · a turning point · to bite the bullet
◆ QUESTION 03 · A TIME YOU FELL FLAT
Tell us about a time you tried something and it didn't go well. What did you learn? Would you do it again?
Try to use: to fall flat on your face · a setback · to bounce back · resilience · in over your head · what I learned
◆ QUESTION 04 · FEAR vs FAILURE
Which is worse — the fear of failing, or actually failing? Why? Which has held you back more in life?
Try to use: self-doubt · imposter syndrome · feel the fear · to play it safe · to overcome · personal growth
◆ QUESTION 05 · COMFORT ZONE = BAD?

Try to use: calculated risk · to play it safe · to embrace · resilience · everyday life · on the other hand
◆ QUESTION 06 · ADVICE TO YOUR YOUNGER SELF

Try to use: to spread your wings · to take guts · a leap into the unknown · nothing ventured nothing gained · to grow as a person

📝Grammar: Conditionals & Verb Patterns

Two B2 grammar areas that come up constantly when talking about challenges and growth: conditionals ("if I'd known…", "if I were braver…") and verb patterns (verb + -ing, verb + to-infinitive).

🚀 Part 1 — Conditionals (real and imagined)

  • 1st conditional — real future. If you don't try, you'll never know.
  • 2nd conditional — hypothetical present. If I were braver, I would quit my job.
  • 3rd conditional — past regret. If I had known, I would have said yes.
  • Mixed — past condition → present result. If I had taken that job, I would be in Berlin now.
  • Advice with I'd + verb: If I were you, I'd just go for it.

🚀 Part 2 — Verb patterns (-ing or to + infinitive)

  • verb + -ing: avoid, enjoy, regret, suggest, mind, can't stand, keep, finish, consider
  • verb + to-infinitive: decide, want, hope, plan, refuse, manage, agree, learn, choose
  • Both possible (different meaning): stop, remember, forget, try
  • e.g. I stopped smoking (= I quit) vs I stopped to smoke (= I paused in order to smoke)
  • e.g. I tried writing (= I experimented with it) vs I tried to write (= I made an effort)
Tip: AVOID, ENJOY, REGRET, SUGGEST → -ing | DECIDE, WANT, HOPE, PLAN → to + infinitive
