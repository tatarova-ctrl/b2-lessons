How the best students actually learn. The highlighted words are your target language — you'll need them below.

The Learning Brain

★ Study skills · How top students really work

Most teenagers assume that the students at the top of the class are simply gifted. The truth is more encouraging: research suggests that how you study matters more than natural talent, and almost anyone can broaden their horizons with the right habits.

Take the most common method of all — cramming. The night before a test, students try to memorise everything at once. It feels productive, but the information fades within days. Psychologists prefer spaced repetition: reviewing material in short sessions over time, just before you forget it.

Another trap is rote learning — repeating facts without understanding them. You might pass the test, but you will struggle to grasp the concept behind it. Real understanding comes from asking why, not just what.

Perhaps the most powerful idea in education is a growth mindset. Students who believe ability can grow tend to bounce back from failure, while those who see it as fixed often give up. A bad grade, in this view, is feedback, not a verdict.

School is not only about marks. Extracurricular activities teach skills no curriculum can, and they help you keep up with a fast-changing world. And learning no longer stops at the school gates: lifelong learning means the most successful people never stop being curious.

💼Employment & Unemployment

What work will really look like for your generation. Target language is in highlighted bold.

Work Isn't What It Used to Be

★ The job market · The world of work you're about to enter

A generation ago, people often had a job for life. They joined one company, slowly climbed the career ladder, and retired with a pension. For today's teenagers, the job market looks completely different.

First, machines are changing everything. Some routine jobs are disappearing as tasks become automated, and workers are made redundant. But new roles appear too — in coding, design, green energy and care. The trick is to keep learning so you don't fall behind.

Second, the way we work has changed. Many people now enjoy remote work, logging in from home instead of commuting to an office. Others join the gig economy, taking short, flexible jobs through apps rather than one steady contract.

With so much change, job security — the feeling that your job is safe — is harder to find. Unemployment can rise quickly when the economy struggles, and being out of work is stressful, both financially and emotionally.

So what helps? Employers increasingly value soft skills — communication, teamwork, problem-solving — as much as qualifications. A strong CV still matters, but the people who land a good job are usually those who can adapt, learn fast and work well with others.

🚄Modern Transportation

The quiet revolution on our roads and rails. Target language is in highlighted bold.

How We Move Is Changing Fast

★ Mobility · The future of getting from A to B

For a century, the world ran on petrol. Today, the way we move is being reinvented — and the goal is to cut emissions without making life slower or more expensive.

The most visible change is the rise of electric vehicles. Quiet, clean and increasingly affordable, EVs are slowly replacing petrol cars and helping drivers shrink their carbon footprint. Charging networks are spreading, though limited range still worries some buyers.

Cities are rethinking the daily commute. Better public transport — buses, trams and especially high-speed rail — can move thousands of people while easing traffic congestion. Many cities now reward cycling and walking over driving.

Then there is the technology that sounds like science fiction: self-driving cars. Powered by sensors and AI, autonomous vehicles promise fewer accidents and more free time — but they raise hard questions about safety and jobs.

Sharing is part of the future too. Through ride-sharing apps, one car can serve many people, cutting the number of vehicles on the road. None of this works, however, without serious investment in infrastructure — the roads, rails, charging points and networks that hold a modern transport system together.

📱Modern Technology

The tools that run — and sometimes ruin — our days. Target language is in highlighted bold.

Life on the Screen

★ Digital life · The technology that shapes every day

No generation has ever been as connected as yours. From the moment you wake up, smart devices shape your day — but the same tools that empower us can quietly take over.

The biggest story is artificial intelligence. AI now writes, draws, translates and answers questions in seconds. It is cutting-edge and genuinely useful, yet it raises real worries about jobs, honesty and who controls it.

Then there is social media. A single clever video can go viral and reach millions overnight. But endless scrolling is deliberately addictive, and constant comparison can damage how young people feel about themselves. Managing your screen time is now a real life skill.

Everything you do online leaves a digital footprint — posts, photos, searches and likes that can follow you for years. Protecting your online privacy, choosing strong passwords and thinking before you share have never mattered more.

Good technology should be user-friendly: powerful but easy to use. And whatever you create, remember one boring but vital habit — always back up your data, because devices break, get lost or get stolen when you least expect it.
