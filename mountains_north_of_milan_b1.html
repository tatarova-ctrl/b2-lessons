⛰️ Mountain Vacations North of Milan

Where to go and what to do in the beautiful Italian Alps, just a few hours north of Milan.

A Week North of Milan

★ Marco Vidal · Travel writer · Lives in Lecco · Writes about the Italian Alps

Milan is a busy, hot, noisy city in summer. But there is a small secret many tourists don't know: two hours north of Milan, you find some of the most beautiful mountains in Europe. Real high mountains, with lakes at the bottom and snow at the top. If you visit Milan and don't get away for a weekend, you miss half the story of this region.

Let's start with Lake Como. It is only one hour by train from Milan. The lake is long and thin, with big mountains on both sides. Small towns like Bellagio, Varenna and Menaggio sit on the water. You can take a ferry, drink a coffee, eat pasta by the lake, and look up at the mountains. Many people come just for the day, but two nights is much better.

“In Milan you check your phone. In the mountains, you look up. That is the whole difference.”

If you want higher mountains, head up to Valtellina. This is a long valley in the north, famous for two things: skiing in winter and cheese and wine in summer. The main town, Sondrio, is small and friendly. In the local restaurants, you can try pizzoccheri, a dark pasta with cheese, potato and cabbage. It sounds strange. It's wonderful.

For serious mountains, keep going to Bormio. Bormio is an old town at 1,225 metres, with hot water baths that are 2,000 years old. In winter it is a big ski resort. In summer it is a base for hiking. From Bormio, the road climbs up to Passo dello Stelvio, at 2,758 metres — one of the most famous mountain roads in the world. Cyclists come from every country to try it.

Then there is Livigno, close to the Swiss border. Livigno is special because you don't pay tax on many things here — petrol, wine, chocolate. So people come here to ski, hike, and buy things cheaply. In summer, Livigno is a paradise for cyclists and walkers. The air is very clean and the sky is very blue.

There are also the Bergamo Alps, east of Como. Here you find quiet valleys, small villages, and hikes for every level. Not many tourists come, so prices are lower and the local people are friendly. Try the Val Brembana or the Val Seriana for a real Italian mountain weekend.

What do people actually do on a mountain holiday? Many things. In summer: hiking, cycling, swimming in cold lakes, eating outside, taking photos, or just sitting with a coffee and doing nothing. In winter: skiing, snowboarding, walking with snow shoes, and sitting by the fire in a mountain hut. Some people come for adventure. Some come to rest. Both are correct.

A big part of a mountain holiday is the rifugio — a small mountain hut where you can eat, sleep, or just take a break. Some rifugios are at 2,500 metres and you can only reach them on foot. The food is simple and delicious. You eat, drink wine, watch the sun go down over the peaks, and sleep in a small warm bed.

If you take one idea, take this. Milan is only the door. The real Italy is one hour north, up in the mountains. Go for one weekend. Make the most of two or three days. Come back for a whole week the next year. The mountains north of Milan are waiting for you.

🥾 Hiking & Walking in the Mountains

How to walk in the mountains safely and happily. Perfect for the trails around Como, Bormio and Val Brembana.

One Step at a Time

★ Sara Colombo · Mountain guide · 15 years walking in the Italian Alps

Hiking is the most beautiful thing you can do in the mountains, and also the most dangerous if you do it badly. After 15 years as a mountain guide, I can tell you the truth: a good hike is not about being strong or fast. It is about being ready, being sensible, and enjoying every step. The mountain is always bigger than you. Respect it, and it will give you the best day of your year.

Let's start with the basics. There are three main types of hike: easy, medium, and hard. An easy hike is one or two hours on a flat path, good for children and grandparents. A medium hike takes 3 to 5 hours and goes up and down. A hard hike is longer than 6 hours, with big height changes and sometimes rocks or snow. Always choose a hike for your real level, not your dream level.

“The mountain is not a competition. Nobody at the top asks how fast you were. They only ask if you enjoyed the walk.”

Now, the gear. You don't need expensive clothes, but you need the right things. Good hiking boots are the most important item — not trainers. A small backpack. Water — at least one and a half litres per person. Food — a sandwich, some nuts, chocolate. A light jacket, even in summer. A hat. Sun cream. And a small first-aid kit.

In the Alps, always check the weather forecast the night before. A sunny morning can turn into a big storm at 2 p.m. If a storm is coming, don't set off. It's not worth it. In Valtellina and Bormio, ask at the tourist office — they know the mountain better than any app.

Then there is the map. Every good hiker carries a paper map, even with GPS on their phone. Batteries die. Phones fall. A paper map does not. In Italy, hiking trails are marked with red and white colours on trees and rocks. If you don't see the marks for five minutes, stop and go back to the last one. Never keep going if you're not sure.

Now, the walk itself. Set off early — 7 or 8 in the morning is perfect. It's cool, the light is beautiful, and you finish before any afternoon storm. Walk slowly. In the mountains, slow is fast. If you can't talk while walking, you are going too fast.

Take breaks often. Every hour, stop for five minutes. Drink water. Eat something small. Look around. This is not just a rule for safety — it's how you enjoy the walk. The best moments of any hike are usually the small breaks.

Try to stop for lunch in a rifugio if you can. In the Alps, most hikes have one after 2 or 3 hours. You can drink hot tea, eat pasta or soup, and rest in the sun. The rifugio owners are usually locals who know every path. Ask them for advice.

The most important rule in mountain walking is this: if the weather changes, if you feel very tired, or if the path becomes dangerous, turn back. Turning back is not failure. It is the sign of a smart hiker. The mountain will still be there next weekend.

If you take one idea, take this. Start with easy hikes. Buy good boots. Check the weather. Set off early. Walk slowly. Take breaks. Keep to the path. And if things go wrong, turn back with no shame. Do this, and hiking will give you the best days of your life — year after year.

🌲 Spending Time in Nature

Why time in the forest, by a lake, or on a mountain quietly changes how you feel.

What the Mountains Give You Back

★ Dr Elena Marchetti · Nature therapist · Studies how the outdoors changes tired minds

Something strange happens when you spend a whole day in the mountains. You start the morning feeling tired, worried, full of small problems. By the afternoon, your shoulders are lower. Your face is softer. Your problems feel smaller. You did nothing special. You just walked, looked at trees, listened to water. But something quietly changed. This is not magic. After many studies, we now know: nature is a real, powerful medicine.

The modern life is a busy life. We look at our phones 100 times a day. We hear traffic, cars, buses, adverts, notifications. Our eyes never rest. Our ears never rest. Our brain is always ready for the next small thing. This is very tiring, and we don't even notice. Then we spend one day in the mountains and, suddenly, we remember what quiet feels like.

“Nature does not give you new energy. It gives you back the energy the city quietly stole from you.”

Doctors in Japan have a name for this: forest bathing. It means spending time slowly in a forest, with no phone, no music, no goal. Just walking, breathing, and looking. Studies show that after two hours of forest bathing, your blood pressure goes down, your stress hormones go down, and you sleep better that night.

You don't have to go to Japan. You can do this in the Bergamo Alps, in Val Brembana, or in any forest near your home. The trick is to slow down. Walk very slowly. Stop often. Breathe in the smell of trees. Look at one leaf carefully. Listen to a bird for a full minute. It sounds silly. It works.

There is also the power of water. Sitting by a river, a lake, or the sea has a special effect on the human mind. Scientists call this the “blue mind”. In the north of Italy, you can sit by Lake Como, Lake Iseo, or Lake Garda for one hour and feel completely different. You don't need to do anything. The water does the work.

There is also the power of silence. Real silence is very rare in modern life. In the high mountains, if you sit alone for five minutes, you can hear silence for the first time in years. It is a beautiful, strange feeling. Some people cry. Some people laugh. Both are normal.

There is also the power of light. Ten minutes of morning sunlight in nature helps your body all day. Your sleep improves. Your mood improves. Your energy improves. This is free. This is easy. Yet most modern people spend their whole morning inside, looking at a phone. Fix this one habit, and you will feel very different in one month.

The good news is that even a small amount of nature helps. You don't have to go to Bormio for a week. Twenty minutes in a park, three times a week, is already very powerful. A short walk near a river. A coffee on a bench under a tree. A weekend in Val Chiavenna once a year. All of this counts.

The bad news is that most of us don't do this. We know nature is good, but we stay inside. We know phones are bad, but we scroll. We know silence is beautiful, but we play music. The mountains are only two hours from Milan, but many people who live in Milan never visit them.

If you take one idea, take this. Once a month, get away from it all. Go to the mountains, a forest, a lake, or a park. Leave your phone in your bag. Walk slowly. Sit still. Breathe deeply. Come home tired and quiet. Do this for one year, and something inside you will change. The mountains are waiting. They are also very patient.

🐿️ Understanding Nature Around You

How to really see the trees, animals and weather when you're outside. Nature becomes twice as beautiful when you understand it.

Learning to Read the Mountain

★ Adam Kowalski · Naturalist · Teaches families to see the mountain, not just walk it

Two people go for the same hike. The first one walks fast, arrives at the top, takes a photo, and comes back. The second one walks slowly, looks at the trees, listens to the birds, and comes back full of small stories. Both were on the same mountain. But they saw two completely different worlds. After 20 years teaching families about nature, I can tell you the truth: understanding nature is a skill you can learn. And once you learn it, every walk becomes twice as beautiful.

Let's start with trees. In the Italian Alps, you don't see one forest. You see three. Low down, until about 1,000 metres, you see oak and chestnut trees. In the middle, from 1,000 to 1,800 metres, you see mostly beech trees, with big soft leaves. Higher up, you see only pine and larch trees. The larch is special: it is the only pine tree that loses its needles in winter. In October, the mountain sides turn gold before the winter comes.

“Nature always speaks. Most people just don't stop to listen. When you learn a few words, the whole mountain becomes a friend.”

Now, animals. Big animals are quiet and shy. You will almost never see a wolf or a bear, but they are there. What you can see, if you look out for them, are: ibex (big mountain goats with long curved horns), chamois (fast, small mountain goats), marmots (fat, funny animals that whistle when scared), foxes, deer, and many birds. Look with your eyes, not your camera. The best moments are seen, not photographed.

How to see animals? Three simple rules. First, walk quietly. Talk in a low voice. Animals hear you 200 metres away. Second, look up. Many birds live in the trees, not on the ground. Third, look for movement. Your eye finds movement more easily than shape.

Now, the weather. In the mountains, weather changes fast. But the mountain always gives you warning signs. If small clouds start to build over the peaks in the morning, a storm is often coming that afternoon. If the wind changes direction suddenly, weather is changing. If you hear thunder far away, count the seconds until the flash: every five seconds is one kilometre. If it is under 30 seconds, the storm is close, and you must find shelter.

Then there are the sounds. Every mountain has its own music. The wind in the pine trees sounds different from the wind in the beech trees. A river is loud in spring, quiet in autumn. Cows have bells around their necks in the summer pastures — you hear them from far away. Birds call in the morning and evening. If you stop and listen for five minutes, you will hear a whole small orchestra.

Then there is the sky. In the mountains, the sky is much more interesting than in the city. In the day, you see clouds, birds, and often the moon. At night, you see thousands of stars — the same stars our grandparents saw. In Livigno or Bormio, on a clear summer night, you can see the Milky Way with your own eyes. It is one of the most beautiful things a person can experience.

There is also the question of respect. When you walk in nature, you are a guest. Don't leave litter. Pick up any rubbish you see, even if it's not yours. Don't pick flowers. Don't feed wild animals — it makes them sick. Don't play loud music. Speak in a normal voice. The mountain gives you so much for free; the least we can do is leave it clean for the next person.

If you take one idea, take this. Next time you go for a walk, don't try to arrive faster. Try to see more. Look at one tree carefully. Listen to one bird for a full minute. Watch one cloud change shape. Nature is not a picture on Instagram. It is a living, breathing world. When you start to understand it, you will never feel bored outside again.
